ShuttleSense

An AI badminton coach that analyses match video, and tells you how confident it actually is.

2025 – nowPython, FastAPI, React 19, OpenCV, MediaPipe, SupabaseRepository

Most club players never get regular coaching. They do have phones. ShuttleSense takes a recording of your match and returns court detection, player tracking, pose-based technique and footwork analysis, rally segmentation, tactical patterns and timestamped coaching notes that link back to the exact moment in the video.

The rule I built it around: never invent a number. Every metric carries a confidence score and a "computed from" note. When the pipeline can't see something, it says so. It won't call a rally a winner or an unforced error, because without shuttle-landing detection it can't know.

It's in a pilot with 30+ players at a community centre. I track uploads, active users and how often they accept a recommendation, and use that to decide what to build next.

  1. Probe
  2. Validate
  3. Normalize
  4. Quality gate
  5. Court detection
  6. Tracking
  7. Pose
  8. Shuttle
  9. Rally
  10. Phases
  11. Shots
  12. Biomechanics
  13. Tactics
  14. Insights
  15. Profile

Fifteen stages, run by a worker that pulls jobs off a Postgres queue. The architecture is split along one line: large video bytes never touch the API. The browser uploads straight to private object storage with resumable uploads, the worker reads from storage, and playback uses short-lived signed URLs. The React app on Vercel and the FastAPI service only exchange small JSON control calls.

The in-app coach answers questions like "why am I losing points at the net?" by retrieving from your own stored analysis with fixed templates. There's deliberately no language model in that path, so it can't hallucinate a statistic. Injury questions are caught before routing and referred to a physiotherapist.

I tested it on 13.6 minutes of openly licensed real badminton footage, from 320×240 school games to 1080p tour broadcast, plus a 14-clip synthetic matrix for lighting, blur, shake and occlusion. I published the bad results alongside the good.

TEG Loop Simulator

Can a data centre's low-grade waste heat make electricity, and store it, without breaking its cooling limits?

Capstone, 2026 – nowHeat transfer, thermoelectrics, battery storage, three.jsRepository

Data centres dump enormous amounts of warm water. Thermoelectric generators (TEGs) can turn a temperature difference straight into electricity, but every degree you take out of the coolant loop is a degree the cooling system notices. Our capstone asks whether recovery is worth it once you respect that.

I built the simulator so the team could trade off 5 cooling architectures and 6 battery chemistries in minutes instead of redoing hand calculations for every design.

Open the live simulator

Every hour of a simulated week is solved as a steady state: the coolant return temperature is found so that the heat exchanger plus the TEGs reject exactly the heat the IT load puts into the loop.

Tube side
Gnielinski (turbulent) and Sieder–Tate (laminar), blended through transition; Petukhov friction factor
Shell side
Kern's method, with temperature-dependent viscosity so the flow regime can change shell to shell
Exchanger
ε-NTU per shell, 1–4 shells in series
TEG
1-D module with Peltier, Fourier and Joule terms; junction temperatures solved iteratively through contact, substrate, interface and heat-sink resistances
Battery
LFP, NMC, LTO, sodium-ion, VRLA or vanadium flow; cycle and calendar fade, replaced at 70% state of health
Economics
Ontario-style time-of-use tariff, payback, NPV and levelised cost

A design only passes if it clears every limit from our proposal, checked at the worst case: peak load and the warmest facility water.

  • Coolant temperature change from the TEGs ≤ 1 °C
  • Added pressure drop ≤ 5% of circuit head
  • Pump power increase < 2%
  • Recovery ≥ 100 W per 25 kW rack
  • Positive net output after fans and pumps
  • Payback ≤ 10 years
  • Hot-junction material limit and battery charge acceptance

The simulation guides a physical prototype: a Bi₂Te₃ TEG on a shell-and-tube heat exchanger, charging a sodium-ion battery through a BMS.

We're using ANSYS CFD and FEA to check coolant selection, geometry and thermal stress, and charge/discharge cycling to measure energy retention, degradation and recharge behaviour. Comparing the model against the test bench tells us what to change before design freeze.

Kronos portfolio dashboard

A forecasting dashboard that grades its own predictions against what the market actually did.

2026Python, PyTorch, Streamlit, yfinance, PlotlyRepository

I forked Kronos, an open-source foundation model for financial candlestick data, and built a six-tab Streamlit dashboard on top of it to track an eight-stock watchlist.

Overview
Risk-weighted allocation, live prices, sector breakdown
Analysis
Candlesticks with MA20/MA50, RSI(14), volume and return distribution
Forecast
30-day OHLCV forecast per ticker, saved as a dated snapshot
Forecast vs actual
Prediction band overlaid on real prices as the days pass
Research
Thesis, catalysts and risks for each stock
Allocation
Weight sliders with share counts and concentration risk

Kronos treats market data like a language. A tokenizer first turns each candlestick (open, high, low, close, volume) into hierarchical discrete tokens. A decoder-only transformer, pre-trained on data from more than 45 exchanges, then predicts the next tokens the way a language model predicts the next word.

  1. Fetch prices
  2. Tokenize candles
  3. Transformer
  4. 30-day forecast
  5. Save snapshot
  6. Compare to actual

The dashboard lets you swap between the mini, small and base model sizes and run on CPU, Apple silicon or CUDA.

A forecast is only interesting if you check it. Every prediction is saved with its date, and the Forecast vs Actual tab pulls live prices and scores the model day by day on mean absolute error, root-mean-square error and directional accuracy — did it at least get the direction right?

It's a learning project about model evaluation, not investment advice.
